Paper trading

Practice the whole routine with fake money before a real dollar is at risk: prep, scoring, the scenario map, brackets, hands off, and the journal. Twenty good paper trades is Gate 0, and passing it is how you go live.

Rule — practice with numbers you could really fund. Set the paper balance, the risk per trade and the size to what you could actually put in a real account. A $100,000 paper account trading 5 contracts teaches you nothing about a $2,500 account trading 1 micro: the losses don't sting, the sizing math is different, and the habits won't carry over. If you'd fund $3,000, practice with $3,000.

Set it up

TradingView

  1. Make a free account at tradingview.com.
  2. Open a chart, then the Trading Panel at the bottom. Choose Paper Trading and connect.
  3. In the paper account's settings, reset the balance to the amount you'd really fund.
  4. Place orders as brackets: a limit entry with the stop and take-profit attached.

OANDA (forex)

  1. Sign up for a demo (practice) account on OANDA's site.
  2. If it lets you choose the starting balance, pick the amount you'd really fund.
  3. Use the same limit-plus-bracket orders you'd use live.

The journal

  • Add an account named Paper, with the same balance and risk % you set on the platform. Every paper trade goes there, so it never mixes with live results.

Trade it like it's live

  • The whole Daily Checklist, every session you practice: Sunday prep, the 7:00 am update, the trade card.
  • A zones only (18+), with the six gates.
  • A scenario map for every instrument before its orders go in.
  • Brackets set in prep, then hands off. SCRAM is still the only early exit.
  • Your real risk size, worked out from your real numbers (Module 2).
  • The daily stop counts. Two losses and you're done for the day, even on paper.
  • No do-overs. A missed fill is logged as missed. A rule broken is logged as broken.

Gate 0: when you're ready to go live

You pass when all three are true:

  1. 20 closed paper A trades in the journal's Paper account.
  2. Plan followed on 90% or more of them.
  3. Daily stop never broken.

Then go live small: 1 micro contract for futures, or the minimum units for forex. From there, Gates 1 and 2 decide when you add B zones and a second contract (Module 7.10).

What paper can't teach you

  • Fills are generous. Simulators usually fill a limit the moment price touches it. Live, you may need price to trade through. Expect live results to be a little worse.
  • Delayed data (TradingView futures, free plan) is fine for prepared orders, but don't use it to judge live price action.
  • Real money feels different. Paper builds the routine; it can't build the nerve. That's why going live starts at 1 micro.
